## Intro

Nothing ruins a skate trip faster than showing up at the gate and finding out your board isn't going where you are. Whether you ride a plain wooden deck or an electric board, the rules that matter split into two completely different categories: security/packing logistics (which apply to every skateboard) and battery regulations (which apply only if yours has a motor). This guide covers both properly, with a source for every claim.

## Part 1: Traveling with a regular (non-electric) skateboard

**Security is a non-issue.** TSA states plainly: *"Skateboards are allowed in carry-on bags. Please check with your airline for any size or weight restrictions."* A normal, battery-free board isn't a security concern anywhere — the only real question is airline policy on size.
*Source: TSA.gov*

**The real obstacle is length, not the rules.** Most airlines cap cabin bags around 45–56 cm on the longest side. A standard skateboard (~80 cm) or longboard (up to 150 cm) simply doesn't fit that — which is why several airlines effectively push regular boards into the hold, not because they're banned, but because they're too long for the cabin. British Airways states this outright: *"Traditional skateboard decks cannot be carried as part of your hand baggage due to their length... must always be carried in the hold."*
*Source: britishairways.com*

**Airline-by-airline, for a regular board:**

| Airline | Carry-on? | Checked? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| American Airlines | Not specified for skateboards | Yes, 1 per passenger | Standard checked bag fee, up to 23 kg |
| Delta | Yes, if it fits carry-on size limits | Yes, must be in a bag | |
| United | Not specified for skateboards | Standard rules apply | |
| JetBlue | Yes, if it fits size requirements | Yes | |
| Southwest | Yes, if it fits under the seat | Yes | Must be stowed wheels-up either way |
| Ryanair | Only if it fits cabin bag (40×20×25 cm) | Yes, for a fee | Or buy an extra seat for it |
| British Airways | No — too long for cabin (56 cm limit) | Yes, hold only | |
| SAS | Not specifically listed | Yes, but treated as unlisted item | Needs approval ≥24h before departure |
| Singapore Airlines | Not specified | Yes, listed by name | "Skateboards/Longboard" is an official category |

*Sources: each airline's own official baggage page, listed individually below*

**How to protect it in the hold:**

![Diagram showing a skateboard broken into deck, trucks, and wheels/bearings, each with packing instructions for checked baggage](./images/packing-diagram.png)

- Pack it in a padded skateboard bag or wrap it in clothing inside a suitcase — American Airlines' own liability policy only covers sports equipment damage if it's in a **hard-sided case with visible external damage**; soft bags are treated as fragile items with essentially no coverage.
*Source: aa.com*
- Consider removing the trucks and wheels and packing them separately — reduces the chance of bent trucks or flat-spotted bearings from rough handling, and can help the deck fit into a smaller/cheaper bag. This isn't required by any airline — it's a practical damage-reduction tip.
- Skate tools with pointed bits can trigger secondary screening if loose in a pocket — keep them in a labeled pouch, ideally in checked luggage.

## Part 2: Electric skateboards — the part most riders get wrong

This is governed by aviation safety regulators, not the airline — no airline can override it, and it doesn't matter how you ask at check-in.

**The universal battery-size rule (FAA/IATA, effectively identical worldwide):**

| Battery capacity | Carry-on | Checked | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–100 Wh | Allowed | Device: yes / Spare: no | No airline approval needed |
| 101–160 Wh | Allowed, with airline approval | Device: yes (approval) / Spare: no | Max 2 spare batteries per person |
| Over 160 Wh | Forbidden on passenger aircraft | Forbidden | Must ship as air cargo under dangerous goods rules |

*Sources: FAA PackSafe (faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/lithium-batteries), TSA.gov, IATA Guidance Document for Passengers Travelling with Lithium Batteries (2026 edition)*

**But here's what actually matters more than the Wh math.** IATA's own 2026 guidance document classifies e-skateboards, e-scooters, and similar devices as "small vehicles" and states directly: *"Most small vehicles have batteries that exceed 160 Wh."* On top of that, five major carriers checked for this article have gone further and ban electric skateboards **outright, regardless of battery size**:

- **Delta**: *"will not accept the transport of balance gliders, hoverboards, powered skateboards, motorized riding suitcases and self-balancing boards of any type which use lithium or lithium-ion batteries... prohibited as both carry-on and checked baggage."*
*Source: delta.com/us/en/baggage/prohibited-or-restricted-items/sporting-leisure-goods*
- **United**: bans "recreational self-propelled vehicles... including smart balance wheels and boards, such as hoverboards, airboards and skateboards."
*Source: united.com — dangerous items page*
- **JetBlue**: explicitly lists "balance gliders, hoverboards, electric skateboards, or self-balancing boards with lithium/lithium ion batteries" as prohibited.
*Source: jetblue.com/help/prohibited-items*
- **Southwest**: "only manual or non-electric/non-motorized skateboards and scooters are accepted."
*Source: southwest.com — official help article*
- **British Airways**: "electric skateboards are not permitted on our aircraft at all, either in the cabin or in the hold, due to the unstable nature of lithium batteries."
*Source: britishairways.com*

**Practical takeaway: if you have an electric skateboard, assume you cannot fly with it as luggage on any of the five airlines above** — don't rely on Wh calculations to save you, because the ban isn't Wh-based for these carriers. Your realistic options are: verify your specific carrier directly rather than assuming, ship the board separately as air cargo under dangerous goods rules, or plan to rent/buy a board at your destination.

## Checklist

- [ ] Regular board: check your airline's carry-on length limit before assuming it'll fit in the cabin.
- [ ] Regular board: use a padded or hard-sided case if you want any damage liability coverage.
- [ ] Regular board: consider removing trucks/wheels for checked baggage to reduce damage risk.
- [ ] Electric board: check your specific airline's policy directly — several ban it outright regardless of battery size.
- [ ] Electric board: if not outright banned, calculate the actual Wh rating before booking anything.
- [ ] Never pack a spare lithium battery in checked baggage — always carry-on only, terminals protected.
- [ ] Pack tools with pointed bits in checked baggage to avoid security delays.
